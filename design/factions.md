# Factions — lore → mechanics → art (v0 writeup)

## The product architecture this unlocks

**Each booster pack IS a faction.** One 32-card pack = one faction's complete game. That gives us a clean ladder:

- **1 pack** = a complete 2-player game (the promise, kept). Both players play out of the same faction pack.
- **2 packs** = the *real* game emerges: faction vs faction, asymmetric matchups, Brawl Stars texture. The second pack is the actual product.
- **N packs** = mix-and-match, house metas, "collect all four." Kickstarter launches with 4 factions; new factions are the stretch-goal / Set 02 engine forever.

This beats the old plan (one neutral pack) on repeatability — 16 cards wasn't much; four 32-card faction decks with mechanical identities is a game system.

## Lore: from two orders to the Concordance

The lore bible's Archivists/Glitchwrights split stays, but widens: as the Glitch spread, *everyone developed a philosophy about it*. The orders together (grudgingly) form the **Concordance** — they meet, they argue, and above all they play, because finished games patch reality. **Signal and Static remain the two sides of any single match** (board-level, like white/black); your *faction* is the school you trained in and the deck you carry. This separation matters: kids pick a faction identity that persists across games, while any two factions can still sit down at one board.

In-world, a booster pack is a **field kit**: the sheaf of pressed glitches, awakened companions, and annotations that a member of that order carries to a match.

## The four launch factions

Each faction = a philosophy of the Glitch + a mechanical identity + a signature keyword + an art dialect (straight from the bake-off — the bake-off didn't have a winner, it had four faction styles).

### 1. The Marginalia (the Archivists' order)
- **Philosophy:** *Read the glitch. Preserve everything.* Librarians of a crumbling reality.
- **Mechanics:** control & memory — Patches, cancels, undo, retrieval from discard, resurrection, protecting squares. The "control" school. Keyword: **Annotate** (mark a square or card now, cash the mark in later).
- **Characters:** the Docent (bishop), the Night Clerk (rook), Pip the Remembering Pawn (the mascot — she remembers the crown).
- **Art dialect:** Archivist's Schematic (D5) — sepia/cyanotype manuscript pages, invented shorthand, red-ink corrections. Palette: prussian blue, parchment, red ink. Sigil: ✱ the annotation mark.

### 2. The Glitchwrights
- **Philosophy:** *Ride the glitch. The world is a build to be patched.* Exploit-hunters, speedrunners, tinkerers.
- **Mechanics:** tempo & movement — teleports, double-moves, phasing, swaps. The "aggro" school. Keyword: **Overclock** (extra movement at a cost).
- **Characters:** Volt (a knight that arrives before it leaves), Wren (queen's engineer), Solder (a pawn with a jetpack that mostly works).
- **Art dialect:** Risograph Arcana (D2) — spot-color misprints, the offset double-image AS the glitch. Palette: fluoro pink, teal, warm black on cream. Sigil: two offset squares.

### 3. The Errantry
- **Philosophy:** *The pieces are waking up. Let them.* An order OF awakened pieces — knights errant of the Lattice who serve no player, only the game.
- **Mechanics:** character-forward — the most/strongest Awakened characters, transformation, promotion tricks, pieces that grow. The "midrange/hero" school. Keyword: **Awaken** (their whole thing; e.g. they can field an extra character).
- **Characters:** the Colossus (the monument knight from the bake-off), Vesper (a bishop that walks off the board and back on elsewhere), the Twins (rooks that mirror each other).
- **Art dialect:** Ligne Claire Cosmos (D3) — monumental Moebius wonder, tiny figures, vast plains. Palette: coral/violet dusk, stone, cyan cracks. Sigil: a horsehead cracked with light.

### 4. The Hush
- **Philosophy:** *The silence is coming. Learn to breathe in it.* Not villains — monks who walk into the Nullity and come back calm. The faction for kids who pick the mysterious one.
- **Mechanics:** denial & erosion — freezes, silences (turn off a character's quirk), removing squares from the board, no-capture zones. The "disruption" school. Keyword: **Null** (a square or ability simply stops).
- **Characters:** the Quiet Queen, Fermata (a pawn the Nullity refuses to take), the Docent's lost brother (graphic-novel bait).
- **Art dialect:** Foil & Fragment (D6) — cream paper, torn fragments, and iridescent blankness where the world is missing. The foil IS the Nullity. Palette: cream, black, holographic. Sigil: an empty square.

*(Stretch factions later: a daemon-keeper faction in the Arcade Corruption style (D4) — jars, pets, chaos, maximum kid energy; a deep-archive "Compilers" faction that plays Events. The style-to-faction mapping means every future aesthetic we like becomes a faction, not a rebrand.)*

## Format v1 (concrete, playtestable)

**Pack (32):** 8 character cards + 20 effect cards + 2 rules cards + 2 reference cards.

**Setup (one pack):** standard chess. Characters out face-up; players alternate picking until each has 2 (draft = the "pick your main" ritual). Shuffle the 20 effects as a shared deck; deal 3 each.
**Setup (two packs):** draft 2 characters from *your* faction; your own 20-effect deck; draw from your own deck.

**Characters ("Awakened"):** play one by attaching it to a matching piece on the board (ring/marker on the piece; card sits face-up in front of you). It grants a **persistent quirk** (always on, public) + a **signature move** (once per game — turn the card sideways when spent). If the piece is captured, the character card is lost with it — characters make you protective, effects make you reckless, and that tension is the game.

**Turn:** play up to one card (character or effect), then move. **Catch-up:** when you lose a piece, draw a card.

**Escalation (the cataclysm dial):** printed on the rules card, faction-agnostic: on each player's 10th, 20th, 30th move, the outermost untouched file goes Null (offline) — the board shrinks, endgames arrive, matches cap at ~25 minutes.

**Faction balance rule of thumb:** every faction must have an answer to every other faction's keyword somewhere in its 20 (Marginalia cancels, Glitchwright dodges, Errantry outgrows, Hush turns off).

## Art system: how four styles stay one product

The unifying conceit — very Robin Sloan — is that **every card is a physical document from inside the world**, collected and cataloged by the Marginalia. A riso misprint, a blueprint, a codex plate, a torn fragment: different documents, one archive. Concretely:

- **One shared card frame** (title bar, type line, faction sigil, text box) across all factions; only the art dialect changes inside the art box.
- **One universal card back:** a Marginalia catalog card (also required so mixed decks shuffle clean). This is the first art commission that matters.
- **Paper is the through-line:** every dialect sits on warm paper stock tones — even the Errantry's plates read as pages from an illustrated codex. (Conveniently, 3 of our 4 bake-off winners already live on cream paper.)

## Open questions / alternative shapes to pressure-test

1. **4 factions vs 3 at launch?** 3 (rock-paper-scissors identity: control/tempo/hero) is tighter and cheaper; 4 gives the Hush, and "the mysterious fourth one" historically sells. Current lean: 4.
2. **Shared-deck solo-pack play vs splitting one pack into two 10-card mini-decks** — playtest; shared deck is simpler and keeps solo-pack games symmetric.
3. **Do characters die with their piece, or return to hand?** Dying is dramatic and creates the protect-your-hero tension, but may be feel-bad for kids. Test both; maybe a Marginalia card resurrects (on-brand).
4. **Does the 8-character draft leave 4 unpicked every game?** Yes — that's replayability, not waste. But check that 2-per-player is the right number vs 1.
5. **Complexity budget:** a faction pack must still teach the whole game from 2 rules cards. The keyword system has to stay at one keyword per faction.
