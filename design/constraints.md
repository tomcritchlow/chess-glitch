# Constraints

The product promise: **one booster pack is a complete game.** Everything else flows from that.

## The pack

- Single SKU: a booster pack of 16 or 32 cards. No base set, no starter box, no expansion-dependency.
- Target price: impulse-buy territory (~$10–15). It should sit at a bookstore or game-store register, not on a shelf of big boxes.
- "Everything you need to play" — the open question is what that means:
  - **Option A — BYO chess set.** Pack = play cards + rules card. Cheapest, but breaks the promise for anyone who doesn't own a board.
  - **Option B — pack includes the board.** Fold-out paper/tyvek board + punch-out standees, plus cards. Strains a booster form factor.
  - **Option C — cards ARE the board/pieces.** Some cards double as board tiles or piece stand-ins (card backs tile into a board, or a "field" card grid). Most elegant, hardest to design.
  - Current lean: A for playtesting, decide A vs C before Kickstarter. Option C is the kind of trick that makes a Kickstarter video sing.

## Card count math (32-card pack)

Rough budget to keep honest:
- 2 rules cards (rules must fit on 2 cards — this is a hard writing constraint)
- 2 reference cards (turn order / card-type key)
- 24–28 play cards
- 0–4 board/piece utility cards (if Option C)

A 16-card pack halves the play cards (~12), which means every card must earn its slot twice over.

## Design constraints on the cards themselves

- Rules text must fit large-type on a poker card. If a card needs a FAQ, it's cut.
- No card may directly win the game (no "capture the king" card). Cards bend the board; chess still decides.
- No memory burden: effects resolve now or leave a visible marker on the board. Nothing players must silently remember for 3 turns.
- Playable with zero chess-theory knowledge. Cards are the beginner's equalizer — chaos compresses the skill gap (Brawl Stars principle: a match should feel winnable by the weaker player and worth winning by the stronger one).
- Kid-friendly, not kid-looking: no gore, no NSFW, no sexualization. Menace and mystery are fine; splatter is not.

## Kickstarter-shaped constraints

- The whole pitch must be explainable in one sentence: *"Chess, but the rules are glitching."*
- Print spec should stay standard poker size, standard card stock, one deck — keeps fulfillment simple and stretch goals cheap (foils, alt art, second pack "Set 02").
- Lore must be real enough to sustain stories/graphic novels (see `lore/lore-bible.md`) — the world is a stretch-goal engine.
