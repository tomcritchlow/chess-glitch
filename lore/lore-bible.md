# Glitch Chess — Lore Bible (v0)

Tone target: literary sci-fi with warmth. Mr. Penumbra's 24-Hour Bookstore meets TRON meets a rainy-afternoon chess club. Mystery and wonder, no gore. A kid can love it; an adult suspects it was written for them.

## The premise

**Chess is the oldest program still running.**

Long before anyone alive can remember, the world was *written* — compiled by builders nobody has names for — and chess was left behind as its diagnostic routine. Sixty-four squares, thirty-two processes, a clean little proof that the world still works. For centuries people played it and thought it was just a game.

Then the world started to glitch.

It began small: a square that flickered. A knight that arrived somewhere it couldn't have gone. A pawn that remembered being a queen. The old game — the *protocol* — was showing the cracks in reality itself. And whoever understood the glitches could use them.

## The two orders

**The Archivists of the Marginalia.** Keepers of the unbound moves. They operate out of the Marginalia itself — a bookshop that exists in the gap between squares, whose shelves are indexed by games that were never finished. Every glitch ever witnessed is written down in the margins of their books, in a shorthand called *tracery*. Archivists believe the glitches are messages — annotations left by the builders — and that the game, played well, is a way of reading them. They are librarians, essentially, of a crumbling reality. Warm, dusty, fiercely protective.

**The Glitchwrights.** Tinkerers, speedrunners, exploit-hunters. Where Archivists *read* the glitches, Glitchwrights *ride* them. They believe the world isn't a text to be preserved — it's a build to be patched, and possibly improved. Goggles, soldering irons, contraband daemon-jars. Not villains: the tension between the orders is curatorial vs. hacker ethics, not good vs. evil. Some of the best players hold dual membership and tell neither side.

**The antagonist pressure** (for stories that need one): **the Nullity** — the spreading blankness where the world's code has fully failed. Not a villain with a face; an encroaching silence. Squares taken by the Nullity simply stop. Both orders, whatever their quarrels, play *against* it — because it turns out that finished games patch reality, a little. Every match matters.

## Why people play (the engine for stories)

A game of glitch chess is a **repair ritual**. Two players sit down over a board where reality is thin, the glitches come out, and — win or lose — the finished game leaves the world slightly more stitched-together than before. Tournament arcs, reluctant-prodigy arcs, odd-couple mentor arcs (an Archivist and a Glitchwright forced to partner), heist arcs (steal a forbidden card from the Marginalia's restricted shelf) all fall out of this naturally.

## The cards (in-world)

Cards are **captured glitches**: a witnessed anomaly, written down in tracery and pressed flat. Playing a card *re-runs* the glitch. This is why a booster pack is a complete game — in-world, a pack is a *field kit*: a sheaf of pressed glitches an Archivist or Glitchwright carries into a match. Card types map to lore:

- **Exploits** — glitches the Glitchwrights harvested
- **Patches** — the Archivists' corrective annotations
- **Daemons** — small persistent programs, kept in jars, released onto the board
- **Events** — the world glitching *on its own*, mid-game

## The board (in-world)

Sides aren't black and white; they're **Signal** (cyan, the world as written) and **Static** (magenta, the world as it's drifting). Every match is Signal v. Static regardless of who sits where — players *channel* a side. This keeps "opponent" from meaning "enemy," which matters for the kid-friendly tone.

## Visual language (feeds `art/prompts.md`)

- Luminous dark: deep indigo-black fields, cyan/magenta light, warm amber accents (lamplight in the Marginalia).
- Clean sci-fi with bookish textures: circuit traces that resolve into marginalia handwriting; scan-lines over vellum; brass and glass next to holograms.
- Pieces are slightly *wrong* in beautiful ways: a knight with a chrome faceplate, a rook trailing pixel-dust, a queen whose crown is a cursor.
- Absolutely no: gore, horror-body imagery, sexualization, grimdark grime.

## Naming glossary

- **the Lattice** — the 64-square substrate; colloquially, reality
- **tracery** — the Archivists' glitch-notation shorthand
- **the Marginalia** — the bookshop between squares
- **unbound move** — any move the base protocol forbids
- **a finished game** — a completed match; also slang for anything that turned out okay
- **the Nullity** — the spreading blankness

## Canon rules for writers

- Glitches are wondrous-uncanny, never gory. The scariest thing in the world is silence (the Nullity), not violence.
- Nobody fully understands the builders. Resist ever revealing them; the mystery is a load-bearing wall.
- Stakes are local and human-scale: a shop, a district, a friendship — the world is saved sixty-four squares at a time.
